## L'Attaque (Standard)

Designed by Hermance Edan, France. Patent filed November 1908 (French patent No. 396,795); published 1910 by Au Jeu Retrouvé; published in Britain by H.P. Gibson & Sons from 1925. The first mass-market hidden-information rank game and direct ancestor of Stratego (1946).

### Board

A **9-column × 10-row** grid (portrait orientation). Three obstacles are placed in the centre of the board: **three 2-square impassable areas** arranged horizontally across the middle rows. No piece may enter an obstacle square.

The three obstacle positions (as shown in the 1909 patent diagram, row 5 or 6 of 10, columns approximately 2, 5, and 8 of 9 — each covering 2 squares vertically or horizontally): these are single-column or single-row narrow obstacles, not the 2×2 block lakes used in Stratego.

Players set up on their own half: rows 1–4 for one player, rows 7–10 for the other. Rows 5–6 are the neutral centre (containing the obstacles).

### Piece Set (36 per player)

Piece names are those of the original French/British edition. These differ from Stratego (1946), which reused the system with different names.

### Setup

Each player places all 36 pieces face-down in the first four rows of their side (rows 1–4 for one player; rows 7–10 for the other), in any arrangement they choose. The opponent cannot see piece ranks. Obstacle squares remain empty throughout the game.

### Movement

Players alternate turns. On each turn, a player moves one piece:

- **Most pieces:** 1 step orthogonally (up, down, left, right). Not diagonally. Not into obstacle squares. Not off the board.
- **Scout:** Slides any number of squares in one orthogonal direction, stopping immediately before any occupied square or obstacle.
- **Mine and Flag:** May not move.

### Attack and Capture

When a piece moves onto a square occupied by an opponent's piece:

1. Both pieces are flipped face-up and ranks are revealed.
2. The lower-ranked piece is permanently removed from the board.
3. Equal rank: both pieces are removed.
4. The winning piece remains on the contested square.

**Special capture rules:**
- **Spy vs. Commander-in-chief:** If the Spy attacks (moves onto the Commander-in-chief's square), the Spy wins. If the Commander-in-chief attacks the Spy, the Commander-in-chief wins.
- **Sapper vs. Mine:** Only a Sapper can capture a Mine. Any other piece moving onto a Mine square is eliminated; the Mine remains in place.
- Capturing the Flag (moving onto its square) immediately ends the game.

### Hidden Information

Piece ranks remain face-down and hidden until revealed in combat. Once a piece has been involved in combat and its rank revealed, it remains identifiable to both players for the rest of the game.

### Win Conditions

- **Capture the opponent's Flag:** immediate win.
- **Leave the opponent with no movable pieces:** if all remaining opponent pieces are Mines and the Flag (none can move), that player wins.
