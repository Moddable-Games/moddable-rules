## Aviation

Published by H.P. Gibson & Sons, London, as a standalone game and as the aerial-warfare component of Tri-Tactics (1932). The game is played on a board representing the Air; a portion of each side is marked as an Aerodrome. The object is not to eliminate the opponent but to land one of your Troop Carriers on the opponent's Aerodrome.

The game is also known by the name of its board: Aerodrome. A later WWII-era edition replaced the original piece names with contemporary aircraft (Spitfire, Hurricane, Messerschmitt, etc.) but used identical rules.

### Board

9 columns × 10 rows. No lake or obstacle squares. Each end of the board has two squares marked as the Aerodrome — these squares may only be entered by Troop Carriers.

### Setup

Each player places 40 pieces face-down on their nearest five rows, arranged at their own discretion. The remaining 2 pieces are placed on any two squares of the sixth row. The two Troop Carriers start on the squares marked as the Aerodrome. Pieces stand with their backs towards the opponent so ranks are hidden.

### Movement

Players alternate turns. On each turn, one piece moves one square — forwards, backwards, or sideways. **Diagonal movement is not permitted**, except for Troop Carriers, which are the only pieces allowed to move diagonally.

No piece may enter the Aerodrome squares except Troop Carriers.

### Combat

Pieces cannot move into a square occupied by another piece. When a piece moves adjacent to an opponent's piece (back to back), the moving player **may choose to attack** by declaring "Attack!" (declaring is optional — the player may instead simply observe the adjacency without attacking).

If an attack is declared:
1. Both pieces are revealed.
2. The lower-valued piece is **brought down** (removed from the board).
3. If both pieces have equal value: both are brought down.
4. The winner remains on the square from which it attacked.

If no attack is declared: neither piece is affected. The opponent cannot then attack using that same piece against the piece that declined to attack — they must first move away.

Auxiliaries have no numerical value; when two Auxiliaries meet (Searchlight vs Searchlight, or Gun vs Gun in normal attack), nothing happens and both remain.

### Troop Carriers (value 4½)

Troop Carriers are the win-condition pieces. They begin on the Aerodrome squares.

- **Diagonal movement**: Troop Carriers are the only pieces permitted to move diagonally.
- **Attack/defence**: When a Troop Carrier attacks or is attacked by any piece of value 4 or lower, the lower-valued piece is brought down normally. When a Troop Carrier is attacked by a piece of value 5 or higher, the Troop Carrier is **driven back to its Aerodrome** (not removed from the board); the attacking piece does not advance.
- **Voluntary return**: Once a Troop Carrier has left the Aerodrome, it may not voluntarily return — it can only re-enter the Aerodrome if driven back by an opposing piece.
- **Troop Carriers vs each other**: If two Troop Carriers meet and one attacks, nothing happens; both remain.
- **Guns**: Anti-Aircraft Guns destroy Troop Carriers normally.
- **Win**: A player wins by moving a Troop Carrier onto one of the opponent's Aerodrome squares.
- **Draw**: If both players lose all their Troop Carriers, the game is a draw.

### Airships (value 5)

Airships fight as normal ranked pieces and additionally can **bomb a Troop Carrier in its own Aerodrome**: if an Airship attacks a Troop Carrier that is sitting on an Aerodrome square, the Troop Carrier is removed from the board entirely (considered bombed). If the Troop Carrier is outside the Aerodrome when attacked by an Airship, it is only driven back to the Aerodrome (standard Troop Carrier behaviour).

### Anti-Aircraft Guns

- **Movement**: Guns may only move within their own half of the board. They cannot enter the opponent's half.
- **Automatic defence**: A Gun cannot initiate a normal attack. However, when any opposing piece moves into the adjacent (back to back) position, that piece is automatically **brought down** — regardless of whether the opposing player declares "Attack!" or not.
- **Bomber exception**: If a Bomber (value 2) moves into the adjacent position, it is **not** automatically brought down. If the Bomber then declares "Attack!", the Gun is removed from the board (put out of action). If no attack is declared, nothing happens and both pieces remain.
- **Gun vs Gun**: When one Gun moves adjacent to another, nothing happens; both remain.
- **Ranging (special ability)**: In conjunction with Searchlights, Guns can shoot down pieces at range without moving. See Searchlights section below.
- **Gun disabling Gun**: A higher-range Gun can disable a lower-range Gun: a 4-mile Gun can disable a 3-mile Gun; a 5-mile Gun can disable a 4-mile Gun (when a Searchlight illuminates the target Gun and it is within range).

### Searchlights

Searchlights can attack any piece. When a Searchlight moves adjacent to an opposing piece and declares "Attack!", two things may happen:

1. **Identity revealed**: The opposing piece must reveal its identity. This information is valuable regardless of what action follows.
2. **Gun ranging**: If an Anti-Aircraft Gun is within range of the illuminated piece (see ranges above), the Gun may immediately shoot the piece down **without moving**. The Gun must be shown to the opposing player to demonstrate it is within range. Intervening squares must all be vacant — except that Observation Balloons and Searchlights are not counted as obstructions for ranging purposes. This shooting takes place as part of the Searchlight's move, before the opponent's next turn.

The Gun must be able to theoretically occupy the square of the targeted piece (i.e. it must be within range).

**Special cases:**
- Searchlight vs Reconnaissance Plane (1): If a Reconnaissance Plane declares "Attack!" on the Searchlight, the Searchlight is removed (put out of action).
- Searchlight vs Searchlight: Nothing happens; both remain.
- Searchlight vs Observation Balloon: Nothing happens; both remain.

### Observation Balloons

Observation Balloons are **permanently stationary** — they never move for the entire game. They serve a similar targeting purpose to Searchlights, enabling Gun ranging.

- When any piece **other than** a Reconnaissance Plane (1) or Bomber (2) moves adjacent to a Balloon: that piece is automatically brought down.
- When a Reconnaissance Plane (1) or Bomber (2) moves adjacent **and declares "Attack!"**: the Balloon is brought down.
- When a Reconnaissance Plane or Bomber moves adjacent **without declaring "Attack!"**: nothing happens.
- Balloon vs Searchlight attack: Nothing happens; both remain.
- Balloons do not count as obstructions when a Gun is ranging.

### Win Conditions

- **Win**: Move a Troop Carrier onto one of the two squares marked as the opponent's Aerodrome.
- **Draw**: Both players lose all their Troop Carriers.
