## Playing the Game

### Ability Scores and Modifiers

Every character has six ability scores: Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma. Each score has an associated modifier used whenever that ability applies to a move.

| Score | Modifier |
|---|---|
| 3 | -3 |
| 4-5 | -2 |
| 6-8 | -1 |
| 9-12 | 0 |
| 13-15 | +1 |
| 16-17 | +2 |
| 18 | +3 |

### Making Moves

Moves are the heart of Dungeon World. A move is triggered by an action taken in the fiction — attacking an enemy in melee, studying a situation closely, acting despite danger. When a trigger occurs, that move's rules apply, whether or not the character intends to trigger it. If you don't want to trigger a move, don't take the action that triggers it — describe your character doing something else instead.

Every move that calls for a roll uses 2d6 plus a modifier. The results break down the same way, move to move:

- **10+** is a strong success — the character does what they set out to, cleanly.
- **7-9** is a success at a cost — the character succeeds, but with a complication, a partial result, or a hard choice attached.
- **6-** is a miss — nothing works out as hoped, and the GM makes a move: often a hard one.

### Moves and Dice

Some moves ask you to roll dice to determine an effect rather than a success — damage dice, for instance. These are separate from the 2d6 roll that determines whether a move succeeds.

### Moves and Equipment

Equipment interacts with moves through its tags — a weapon might grant +1 damage, or have the precise tag that changes what stat you use to attack with it. See Equipment for the full list of tags and their effects.

### The Effects of Moves

Moves have all kinds of effects, but a few recur often enough to deserve explanation:

**Deal damage.** The target loses HP equal to the damage dealt, after subtracting armor.

**Take +1 forward.** The bonus applies to the next move you make, then disappears whether you used it or not.

**Take +1 ongoing.** The bonus applies to every relevant roll until the condition granting it ends.

**Give hold.** Hold is a resource, tracked as a number of points, that can be spent later (often 1 point per use) to trigger a specific listed effect. Unspent hold is usually lost at the end of the scene or when the fictional position that grants it ends.

**Present a choice.** Some moves, especially on a 7-9, ask the player to choose between several options instead of granting a clean success — this represents the character succeeding, but not without a cost or a complication.

**Spout lore.** The GM tells the player something true and useful (or merely true) about the subject of their inquiry.

**Mark XP.** The character checks off a box of experience. When they've marked enough XP (level+7 or more), they can level up.

### Harm and Healing

**HP.** Hit Points measure how much punishment a character can take before dying. HP is reduced by damage and restored by healing, rest, and certain moves.

**Damage.** When a character takes damage, subtract their armor from the amount, then subtract the remainder from their HP.

**Damage From Multiple Creatures.** When multiple enemies attack at once as part of a single move, add their damage together and apply it as one hit (unless the GM decides they act separately).

**Stun Damage.** Some effects deal stun damage instead of ordinary damage: it works exactly like damage, but instead of harming the body it overloads the mind or senses, usually with a fictional effect like unconsciousness attached rather than death.

**Adding and Subtracting Damage.** When multiple effects modify a single damage roll, add all the bonuses and penalties together before rolling, or apply them to the result — whichever the specific move indicates.

**Best and Worst.** When told to roll damage dice twice and take the better or worse result, roll both dice pools and pick the higher or lower total as instructed.

**Healing.** Healing restores HP, up to the character's maximum. It comes from magic, potions, rest (Make Camp, Recover), and other specific sources.

**Death.** A character reaches 0 HP takes their Last Breath (see Special Moves). Death isn't guaranteed, but it's always a real possibility.

**After Death.** A dead character's player creates a new character, introduced into the story at the GM's discretion — often at a natural pause in the action.

**Debilities.** Certain effects (usually from a miss on Last Breath or an ongoing curse or wound) impose a lasting debility rather than direct damage:

- **Weak** (-1 Str), **Shaky** (-1 Dex), **Sick** (-1 Con), **Stunned** (-1 Int), **Confused** (-1 Wis), **Scarred** (-1 Cha).

Debilities are removed by the Recover move (three days of rest, or two under a healer's care) or by specific magic.

### Magic

Spellcasting classes (the cleric and the wizard) follow a cycle: **know** a set of spells (the cleric's granted by their deity's domain, the wizard's inscribed in a spellbook), **prepare** a subset of those spells for the day (via Commune or Prepare Spells), **cast** a prepared spell (via Cast a Spell, rolling to see whether it's cleanly successful, costly, or lost), and **forget** — a cast spell may be used up, requiring the character to prepare again before casting it a second time, depending on the roll.

### Character Change

**Level Up.** When a character has downtime and enough XP (their current level+7), they level up: subtract that XP cost, increase level by 1, choose a new advanced move, and increase one ability score by 1 (to a maximum of 18).

**Multiclass Moves.** Several advanced moves (Multiclass Dabbler, Initiate, Master) let a character take a move from another class's list, treating their level as one lower for the purpose of choosing it.

**Requires and Replaces.** Some advanced moves list a requirement (another move you must already have) or note that they replace an earlier move outright, superseding its effect.

**Beyond 10th Level.** The moves presented in this book cover levels 1-10. Continued play beyond that is left to the GM and group to handle as they see fit.

### Bonds

Each character starts with a handful of bonds — statements filling in the blank with another character's name, describing a relationship (trust, debt, suspicion, tutelage, and so on). Bonds tie the party together mechanically (via Aid or Interfere) and fictionally.

**Resolving Bonds.** At the End of Session move, a player may declare a bond resolved (fully explored or no longer relevant), and with the agreement of the other named character's player, mark XP and write a new bond with anyone they choose.

**Writing New Bonds.** New bonds should say something true about how the character sees their companions — trust, rivalry, debt, tutelage, suspicion — and can be revised in play as relationships change.

### Alignment

Each character chooses an alignment (the options vary by class: Good, Neutral, Chaotic, Evil, or Lawful) with a listed condition. Fulfilling that condition at least once in a session lets the character mark XP at the End of Session move.

**Good:** endanger yourself to help another, in the ways your class's alignment text specifies.
**Lawful:** uphold order, law, or a code, in the ways your class's alignment text specifies.
**Neutral:** act to preserve balance or avoid entanglement, in the ways your class's alignment text specifies.
**Chaotic:** disrupt order or act unpredictably, in the ways your class's alignment text specifies.
**Evil:** cause harm or corruption in pursuit of your own ends, in the ways your class's alignment text specifies.

**Changing Alignment.** A character can change their alignment at any natural pause in the action, with the GM's agreement, if the fiction supports it.

### Hirelings

Hirelings are NPCs employed to help on adventures. Each has a Skill, a Cost (in coin per week), and a Loyalty rating.

**Making a Hireling.** Choose a cost appropriate to their skill and danger of the work, and a starting Loyalty (usually 0, adjusted by treatment and reputation).

**Costs.** Simple labor might cost as little as 1 coin a week; skilled or dangerous work costs considerably more, at the GM's discretion.

**Skills.** Common hireling skills include:

- **Adept:** assists with magic or lore.
- **Burglar:** picks locks, disables traps, and steals.
- **Minstrel:** entertains and spreads (or gathers) rumors.
- **Priest:** provides minor divine services and healing.
- **Protector:** fights to defend the party.
- **Tracker:** finds paths and quarry in the wild.
- **Warrior:** fights alongside the party in melee.

### The Adventurer's Life

**Dungeons.** Ancient, dangerous, and full of treasure — dungeons are the classic proving ground for Dungeon World heroes, from ruined tombs to monster warrens.

**Monsters.** The threats that populate the world, from a single goblin to an ancient dragon — see Monsters for the full bestiary and the rules for building your own.

**Wilderness.** The untamed lands between settlements, full of danger, resources, and mystery — see The World and Undertake a Perilous Journey.

**Friends and Enemies.** NPCs — friendly, hostile, or somewhere in between — populate the world and give the characters people to fight for, against, or alongside.

**The World.** Dungeon World's setting is built collaboratively, at the table, through the GM's prep and the players' choices — see The World and Fronts for the tools that make this work.
