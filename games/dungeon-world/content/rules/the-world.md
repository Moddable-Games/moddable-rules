## The World

Dungeon World's setting isn't handed to you complete — it's built at the table, through prep and through play, growing outward from the places the characters actually visit.

### Steadings

A steading is any settled place — a village, town, keep, or city — where people (or whatever passes for people) live under some measure of order and safety. Steadings are where characters resupply, recruit, carouse, and get entangled in the schemes of the folk who live there.

### Creating the World

Start your campaign map with a small number of steadings — just enough to give the characters somewhere to start and somewhere to go. Add detail as play demands it: when the characters ask about a place, or head toward the unknown parts of the map, fill in what's there. Leave blanks. A map that's already complete gives you nowhere to grow.

### While You're In Town

Steadings are where downtime moves happen — Carouse, Supply, Recruit, Recover, Outstanding Warrants. They're also where fronts intersect with daily life: a corrupt guard captain, a missing shipment, a whispered rumor about the ruins to the north.

### Elements of a Steading

Every steading has tags describing its **Prosperity**, **Population**, **Defenses**, and any number of **Other** tags describing what makes it distinct.

**Prosperity** ranges from poor to wealthy, describing the general economic condition of the place. **Population** describes roughly how many people live there — tiny hamlets to sprawling cities. **Defenses** describes what stands between the steading and its enemies — nothing at all, a militia, a garrison, walls, or more.

**Other tags** are a glossary of descriptive elements you can mix and match:

- **Safe** — unusually free of danger and crime.
- **Religion** — a notable temple, cult, or faith is centered here.
- **Exotic** — strange goods, customs, or inhabitants not found elsewhere.
- **Resource** — produces something valuable in quantity.
- **Need** — lacks something important, creating opportunity for adventurers.
- **Oath** — bound by an old pact or vow.
- **Trade** — a hub of commerce.
- **Market** — a significant marketplace draws merchants from afar.
- **Enmity** — at odds with a neighboring steading or faction.
- **History** — an important past event still shapes the place.
- **Arcane** — wizardry or arcane study is prominent here.
- **Divine** — touched by, or in service to, a deity.
- **Guild** — a powerful guild or professional order holds sway.
- **Personage** — home to someone notable or influential.
- **Dwarven** — built by, or home to, dwarves.
- **Elven** — built by, or home to, elves.
- **Craft** — known for skilled artisans or a particular trade.
- **Lawless** — order has broken down, or never took hold.
- **Blight** — afflicted by disease, curse, or corruption.
- **Power** — seat of significant political or military power.

### Adding Steadings

Add a new steading to the map whenever the fiction calls for one — the characters ask about a place, travel toward one, or a front's cast needs somewhere to call home. Give it a name and a small number of tags, and let the rest fill in through play.

### Steading Names

A few name fragments to combine or draw from: Hollow, Bright, Stone, Iron, Fen, Wolf, Thorn, Raven, Grey, White, Black, Long, mere, ford, wick, haven, holt, reach, gate, watch, fell, marsh, spire, hold.

### Making a Village

Default tags: small Population, poor or middling Prosperity, no Defenses (or at most a militia). Choose one:

- A resource everyone depends on
- An old oath the village still honors
- A history no one talks about

Choose one problem:

- Something's been stealing livestock
- The harvest failed
- A stranger has taken up residence nearby

### Making a Town

Default tags: middling Population, middling Prosperity, a militia or small garrison. Choose one:

- A market that draws trade from the region
- A guild that controls a trade
- A religious presence with real influence

Choose one problem:

- Rival factions are jockeying for control
- Something valuable has gone missing
- An old enmity has resurfaced

### Making a Keep

Default tags: small Population, a garrison, Defenses of real substance. Choose one:

- A personage of note holds the keep
- The keep guards something important
- An oath binds the keep to a distant power

Choose one problem:

- The garrison is stretched too thin
- Someone inside is not what they seem
- A siege, real or rumored, looms

### Making a City

Default tags: large Population, wealthy or middling Prosperity, walls and a proper garrison. Choose one:

- Multiple guilds vie for influence
- A seat of real political power
- Exotic goods and inhabitants from across the world

Choose one problem:

- Corruption runs through the halls of power
- A blight, plague, or curse festers beneath the surface
- Factions within the city are close to open conflict

### Fronts on the Campaign Map

Mark where a front's dangers are located on the campaign map. As grim portents come to pass, update the map to reflect the changing world — a steading under siege, a road grown too dangerous to travel, a new faction's banner over a keep.

### Updating the Campaign Map

As the characters interact with the world (or ignore it), steadings and regions change. Some ways the map might update between sessions:

- **Growth** — a steading's Population or Prosperity tag improves.
- **Collapse** — a steading loses tags, shrinks, or is abandoned.
- **Want** — a steading gains a Need tag reflecting a new shortage.
- **Trade** — new trade routes connect steadings, or old ones dry up.
- **Capture** — a steading's Defenses fail and it falls to a hostile power.
- **Profit** — a steading's Prosperity improves due to the characters' actions.
- **Surplus** — a Resource tag's benefits spread to neighboring steadings.
- **Aid** — a steading sends help to another in need.
- **Embattled** — a steading gains an Enmity tag with a new rival.
- **Opportunity** — a new Need or Resource tag creates an opening for adventurers.
- **Clash** — two steadings' interests come into open conflict.

### Other Updates

Beyond steadings, update the wider map: new dungeons discovered, roads made safe or dangerous, wilderness regions claimed by monsters or reclaimed by settlers. The map should always reflect a world in motion, shaped by (and shaping) the characters' adventures.
