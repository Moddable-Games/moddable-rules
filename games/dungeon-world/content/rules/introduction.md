## Introduction

Dungeon World is a game of fantasy adventure. It's about heroes who explore magical, mysterious, and cruel lands. Fighters, wizards, clerics, and rogues (to name a few) band together against the evils that pervade the fantastic world they inhabit. Dungeon World is played with your imagination, aided by dice and numbers, but always focused on the action, intrigue, and mystery of a fantasy story.

### What Do the Heroes Do?

The adventurers of Dungeon World come from many backgrounds and callings, but each character presented has a role to fill.

**The bard** weaves the tales of adventurers into song, and finds themselves the hero of more than one story they'll later tell. Masters of manipulation and awareness, they can talk their way into (or out of) almost anything.

**The cleric** is a servant, in body and soul, to a deity that grants divine magic to defend their faith and destroy their god's enemies. They walk the line between two callings: the fury of divine power and the kindness and mercy of healing magic.

**The druid** channels the primal forces of the natural world. They can take the shapes of feral beasts, granting them a dangerous versatility. They serve as the voice of the natural world in a genre that is often focused on taming or conquering nature.

**The fighter** is a student of the blade (or axe, or hammer). More than any other class, fighters are defined by combat prowess and phenomenal ability to deal (and take) damage. Whether a knight, a barbarian mercenary, or a solitary bandit, the fighter is a fantastic force in battle.

**The paladin** has dedicated their sword, and their life, to a divine cause. Blessed with power to defend their beliefs, the paladin is a group's guardian, healer, and moral compass, all at once.

**The ranger** is a hunter of the wilds. They know the wilderness and its many dangers, and have the skill to defeat those dangers. Their years living outside the cities and towns of civilization give them a suspicion of civilized ways, and their bond with an animal companion gives them a partner as capable as they are.

**The thief** knows how to get into (and out of) places they shouldn't, steal things that aren't theirs, and generally live outside the law. Masters of traps and locks, backstabbing foes, and poisons, thieves are indispensable to any dungeon delving party.

**The wizard** commands magic through steadfast study and diligent research, giving them potent and versatile abilities. Wizardly magic can call forth fire and lightning, glimpse into the future, or even reshape reality itself.

### Why?

Dungeon World is designed to feel like the fantasy you already know and love, but with a very particular focus and set of goals. Foremost, this is a game about characters, not the world or the rules. The characters engage in a story about daring-do and dangerous monsters, ancient mysteries and forgotten treasures. The rules exist to make that story exciting, terrifying, and true to the fiction of adventure, without needing a rulebook to tell you what a dragon is like or how a magic sword must act. Instead, the game trusts you, the players and GM, to know and honor these things while providing structure to make sure the danger of a dragon or the wonder of a magic sword are always present.

Dungeon World is built with the same tools as *Apocalypse World*, from which it borrows the underlying engine. Where *Apocalypse World* portrays a post-apocalyptic world of scarcity and violence, Dungeon World portrays the swords, sorcery, and dragons of traditional fantasy gaming.

### How to Use This Book

This book is broken into a few major sections. Playing the Game covers the core mechanics that everyone at the table needs to know: how moves work, harm, magic, leveling up, bonds, and alignment. The Basic Moves and Special Moves chapters detail the moves every character has access to, regardless of class. Character Creation walks through building a character step by step, and the eight class chapters that follow give each class's unique moves, gear, and options.

The GM, Fronts, and The World chapters are written for whoever is running the game — they cover the GM's agenda and principles, how to build the looming dangers (fronts) that drive a campaign, and how the world itself is built at the table through play. First Session and Example of Play give concrete guidance and a worked transcript for getting a game off the ground. Advanced Delving covers hacking the game itself: writing new moves, new classes, and adapting existing material. Equipment and Monsters provide the gear list and the bestiary, organized by the strange places their inhabitants call home.

### Setting Up

Dungeon World needs a few things to play: this book (or the moves and character sheets within it), a few six-sided dice (2 per player is plenty), pencils and paper for notes and character sheets, and a group of friends. One person takes on the role of GM, portraying the world and its inhabitants; everyone else creates and plays a hero.

### What's Dungeon World Like to Play?

A session of Dungeon World is a conversation. The GM describes the situation the characters find themselves in, and asks "what do you do?" The players describe what their characters do in response, in the fiction — not "I use my Volley move," but "I draw my bow and put an arrow through the goblin's throat." When an action matches the trigger of a move, that move's rules kick in: sometimes a roll is called for, and the result shapes what happens next. The GM never rolls dice; instead, on a miss (or when the fiction calls for it), the GM makes a move of their own — introducing a complication, revealing a threat, dealing damage — always following from what's already been established and always ending with "what do you do?"

This loop — description, action, move, consequence — is the whole of Dungeon World's engine. It produces play that feels like a story being discovered together, with rules that step in exactly when they matter and get out of the way otherwise.
