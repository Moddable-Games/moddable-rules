## Fronts

A front is a way to organize the various threats and dangers in your Dungeon World game — the looming badness that the characters will face if they don't act, and the tool that helps you prep between sessions without planning a fixed plot.

### Making a Front

A front is made up of one or more **dangers**, a shared **description and cast**, one or more **grim portents**, an **impending doom**, and **stakes questions**. Some fronts are Campaign Fronts — sprawling, sweeping, taking a long while to unfold across many sessions — and others are Adventure Fronts, tighter and more immediate, resolved in a session or a few.

Create a front when you need one: to start a game, to give shape to a looming danger the characters have stumbled into, or to organize threats you've been improvising in play. Don't create fronts you don't need — not every monster or NPC needs to belong to one.

### Creating Dangers

A danger is a single monster, location, cult, faction, or other force with its own motives. Choose a category and build from there:

**Ambitious Organizations.** Groups pursuing power. *Impulse: to gain influence.* GM moves: reveal a scheme already underway, show the reach of the organization, offer a corrupting bargain.

**Planar Forces.** Things not of this world. *Impulse: to bridge the planes.* GM moves: show a planar intrusion, offer forbidden knowledge at a cost, twist something familiar into something wrong.

**Arcane Enemies.** Wizards, sorcerers, and their creations. *Impulse: to unlock forbidden power.* GM moves: reveal the cost of the enemy's power, show a magical working already in motion, turn magic against the characters.

**Hordes.** Overwhelming numbers. *Impulse: to overrun.* GM moves: show their numbers, reveal a coordinated advance, sacrifice the weak to protect the strong.

**Cursed Places.** Locations gone wrong. *Impulse: to spread its curse.* GM moves: show the place's influence growing, reveal what the place has already claimed, twist the land itself against the characters.

### Description and Cast

Every front needs a description — what it looks like, what it's already done, how the characters might have encountered it — and a cast of named individuals tied to its dangers. Give at least the most important members of that cast names, motivations, and a role to play.

### Custom Moves

Many fronts benefit from a custom move or two, describing a specific effect tied to a danger — what happens when characters enter the cursed grove, or what a specific ritual does when interrupted.

### Grim Portents

Grim portents are the concrete steps a front will take if the characters don't intervene — the visible signs that the impending doom draws closer. List several for a front, roughly in the order they'll occur absent interference, and reveal them to the players as the story develops (they don't have to happen in order, and the characters' actions may skip, delay, or accelerate them).

### Impending Doom

The impending doom is what happens if every grim portent comes to pass unchecked — the front's ultimate, worst-case outcome. Broad categories of impending doom include:

- **Tyranny** — a ruler or power seizes total control.
- **Pestilence** — disease, blight, or plague spreads unchecked.
- **Destruction** — a place or people is physically annihilated.
- **Usurpation** — rightful power is stolen or replaced.
- **Impoverishment** — a place is stripped of what sustains it.
- **Rampant Chaos** — order breaks down entirely.

### Stakes

Stakes questions are open questions about how the front will resolve, meant to be answered through play rather than decided in advance — "Will the cult complete its ritual before the characters intervene?" "Who will rule the city when the tyrant falls?" Ask one or two per front and leave room to find out.

### Resolving a Front

A front resolves when its impending doom comes to pass, or when the characters have thoroughly dismantled its dangers such that it no longer poses a threat. Either way, look back at your stakes questions and answer them based on what happened in play.

### Multiple Adventure Fronts

A campaign will often have several fronts running simultaneously — a slower campaign front in the background, and one or more adventure fronts the characters are actively engaged with. Don't feel obligated to advance every front every session; let the ones the characters engage with take center stage.

### An Example Front: The Opening of the White Gate

**Danger 1: The Cult of the White Gate** (Ambitious Organization). *Impulse: to open the White Gate and bring their patron through.* Cast: Vexira, the cult's high priestess; Toll, a captured guardsman turned reluctant convert.

**Danger 2: The White Gate** (Cursed Place). *Impulse: to widen the crack between worlds.* The gate itself, a ring of white stone in the hills above the town, already leaking a faint unnatural cold.

**Danger 3: What Waits Beyond** (Planar Force). *Impulse: to bridge the planes.* Whatever the cult's patron actually is — left deliberately undefined until play reveals it.

**Grim Portents:**

1. Livestock near the gate are found frozen solid, inside and out.
2. Vexira completes the first of three rites; the gate's cold begins seeping into the town itself.
3. The cult abducts a handful of townsfolk for the second rite.
4. The second rite completes; the gate's ring of stone cracks, and things begin slipping through at night.
5. The cult moves openly, having lost patience with secrecy, and prepares the third and final rite.
6. The White Gate opens.

**Impending Doom:** Rampant Chaos — whatever comes through the White Gate does not answer to the cult, or to anyone.

**Description and Cast:** The town of Hollowmere sits in the shadow of the hills where, a generation ago, a lesser noble house dabbled in planar magic and vanished. The cult recruits from the town's disaffected and desperate, promising communion with something greater.

**Custom Move:** When you touch the White Gate's stone ring, roll+WIS. ✴On a 10+, you glimpse what's on the other side clearly. ✴On a 7-9, the glimpse is fragmentary and cold seeps into your bones — take -1 ongoing until you Recover.

**Stakes:** Will the characters learn what the cult's patron actually is before the gate opens? Who among the cult's captives can still be saved?
