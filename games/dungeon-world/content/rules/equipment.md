## Equipment

The musty tombs and forgotten treasure troves of the world are filled with useful items. Most items are mundane — not magical or intrinsically unique in any way. Any item that is magical or one-of-a-kind is not mundane for the purposes of moves; the fighter's signature weapon, for instance, is never mundane.

Each piece of equipment has a number of tags describing how it affects a character (like +Armor) or how it's used (like the range tags). These guide the fiction: an awkward weapon might mean you're more likely to drop it when you fail a Hack and Slash roll. This isn't an exhaustive list — feel free to create your own tags.

### General Equipment Tags

*Applied:* Only useful when carefully applied to a person or something they eat or drink.
*Awkward:* Unwieldy and tough to use.
*+Bonus:* Modifies effectiveness in a specified situation ("+1 forward to spout lore," "-1 ongoing to hack and slash").
*n coins:* How much it costs to buy, normally. If the cost includes "-Charisma," subtract the haggler's Charisma score (not modifier) from the price.
*Dangerous:* Easy to get in trouble with; the GM may freely invoke consequences of foolish handling.
*Ration:* Edible, more or less.
*Requires:* Only useful to certain people; works poorly, if at all, otherwise.
*Slow:* Takes minutes or more to use.
*Touch:* Used by touching it to the target's skin.
*Two-handed:* Takes two hands to use effectively.
*n weight:* Count the listed amount against your load. Something with no listed weight isn't designed to be carried.
*Worn:* To use it, you have to be wearing it.
*n Uses:* Can only be used *n* times.

### Weapons

Weapons don't kill monsters, people do — that's why weapons in Dungeon World don't have a listed damage. A weapon is useful primarily for its tags, which describe what it's useful for: a dagger is small and easy to strike with at close distance, not simply "more" or "less" damaging than another blade.

**Weapon Tags:** *n Ammo* (counts as ammunition for appropriate ranged weapons; the number represents what you have left on hand, not individual arrows). *Forceful* (can knock someone back a pace, maybe off their feet). *+n Damage* (add *n* when you deal damage). *Ignores Armor* (don't subtract armor from damage taken). *Messy* (does damage in a particularly destructive way). *n Piercing* (subtract *n* from the enemy's armor for that attack). *Precise* (use DEX to hack and slash with this weapon, not STR). *Reload* (takes more than a moment to reset after attacking). *Stun* (deals stun damage instead of normal damage). *Thrown* (throw it to hurt someone; can't mark off ammo on a 7–9 with Volley, since once thrown it's gone until recovered).

**Range tags:** *Hand* (attacking something within your reach, no further). *Close* (arm's reach plus a foot or two). *Reach* (several feet away, maybe as far as ten). *Near* (as far as you can see the whites of their eyes). *Far* (shouting distance). Dungeon World doesn't penalize or bonus "optimal range" — but a Hand weapon against an enemy ten yards off is hard to justify using.

### Armor

Armor is heavy, difficult to wear and is damned uncomfortable. Some classes are better trained to ignore these drawbacks, but anyone can strap on a suit of armor and enjoy the benefits it grants.

**Armor Tags:** *n Armor* (subtract it from damage taken; if you have more than one item with n Armor, only the highest counts). *+n Armor* (protects and stacks with other armor). *Clumsy* (-1 ongoing while using it; cumulative).

### Dungeon Gear

**Adventuring Gear** (5 uses, 20 coins, 1 weight) — A collection of useful mundane items such as chalk, poles, spikes, ropes, etc. When you rummage through it for a useful mundane item, you find what you need and mark off a use.

**Bandages** (3 uses, slow, 5 coins, 0 weight) — When you have a few minutes to bandage someone's wounds, heal them of 4 damage and expend a use.

**Poultices and Herbs** (2 uses, slow, 10 coins, 1 weight) — When you carefully treat someone's wounds with these, heal them of 7 damage and expend a use.

**Healing Potion** (50 coins, 0 weight) — When you drink an entire healing potion, heal yourself of 10 damage or remove one debility, your choice.

**Keg of Dwarven Stout** (10 coins, 4 weight) — When you open a keg and let everyone drink freely, take +1 to your Carouse roll. Drinking a whole keg yourself leaves you very, very drunk.

**Bag of Books** (5 uses, 10 coins, 2 weight) — When it contains just the right book for what you're spouting lore on, mark off a use and take +1 to your roll.

**Antitoxin** (10 coins, 0 weight) — When you drink it, you're cured of one poison affecting you.

**Dungeon Rations** (Ration, 5 uses, 3 coins, 1 weight) — Not tasty, but not bad either.

**Personal Feast** (Ration, 1 use, 10 coins, 1 weight) — Ostentatious to say the least.

**Dwarven Hardtack** (Requires Dwarf, ration, 7 uses, 3 coins, 1 weight) — Dwarves say it tastes like home. Everyone else says it tastes like home, if home is a hog farm, and on fire.

**Elven Bread** (Ration, 7 uses, 10 coins, 1 weight) — Only the greatest of elf-friends are treated to this rare delicacy.

**Halfling Pipeleaf** (6 uses, 5 coins, 0 weight) — When you share it with someone, expend two uses and take +1 forward to parley with them.

### Poisons

**Oil of Tagit** (Dangerous, applied, 15 coins, 0 weight) — The target falls into a light sleep.

**Bloodweed** (Dangerous, touch, 12 coins, 0 weight) — Until cured, whenever the afflicted rolls damage, they roll an additional d4 and subtract that result.

**Goldenroot** (Dangerous, applied, 20 coins, 0 weight) — The target treats the next creature they see as a trusted ally, until proved otherwise.

**Serpent's Tears** (Dangerous, touch, 10 coins, 0 weight) — Anyone dealing damage against the target rolls twice and takes the better result.

### Magic Items

There are stranger things in the world than swords and leather. Magic items are the non-mundane items that have intrinsic power. They're for the GM to make for your game, introduced as spoils of battle or rewards for jobs and quests; players can also make them through the wizard's Ritual and similar moves. When making your own, keep in mind that magic items are *magical* — simple modifiers like +1 damage are the realm of the mundane, so magic items should provide more interesting bonuses.

A sampling from the core rules: **Argo-Thaan, Holy Avenger** (a blade of gold, silver and light; in a paladin's hands it increases damage to d12 and grants access to every paladin move, and can harm any Evil creature regardless of defenses). **Bag of Holding** (larger inside than out, infinite capacity, always 0 weight; retrieving an item is roll+WIS, 10+ it's right there, 7-9 choose the exact item slowly or a similar item quickly). **Vorpal Sword** (close, 3 piercing, 2 weight; when you deal damage, the enemy must choose something — an item, an advantage, a limb — and lose it, permanently). **Immovable Rod** (press the button and it sticks in place, immovable, until pressed again). **Nightsider's Key** (unlocks any door you don't belong behind, so long as you go undetected and take nothing but memories). Dozens more — Arrows of Acheron, the Burning Wheel, Cloak of Silent Stars, the Epoch Lens, Farsight Stone, and others — round out the treasury; invent your own freely in the same spirit.
