## Grasshopper Chess

A chess variant featuring the **Grasshopper**: a fairy chess piece that moves along queen lines but *must* hop over exactly one piece (the hurdle) and land on the square immediately beyond it. The Grasshopper cannot move at all if no hurdle is available in its direction.

Invented by T. R. Dawson in 1913 and first published in *Fairy Chess Review*, the Grasshopper is one of the most widely studied fairy chess pieces. It grows stronger as the board fills and weaker as pieces are traded off.

### The Grasshopper Piece

The Grasshopper moves along any queen line (rank, file, or diagonal). It must pass over exactly one piece of either colour (the **hurdle**) and land on the square immediately beyond the hurdle. If that landing square is occupied by an enemy piece, the Grasshopper captures it. If the landing square is occupied by a friendly piece, the move is illegal. If there is no piece to hurdle in a given direction, the Grasshopper cannot move in that direction.
