## Standard Xiangqi

Xiangqi (象棋, Elephant Chess) has been played in China since at least the 7th century and is one of the most popular board games in the world today. The game is played on the intersection points of a 9×10 grid, divided by a river into two halves. The Cannon, which must jump over a piece to capture, and the Flying General rule, which prevents the two Generals from facing each other on an open file, are the mechanics that define the game’s character.

{{svg:xiangqi-board.svg "Standard Xiangqi — starting position"}}

### The Board

The board is a 9×10 grid of intersecting lines. Pieces are placed and moved on the 90 intersection points, not inside squares. The board is divided horizontally between ranks 5 and 6 by the river (河界). A 3×3 palace, marked by diagonal lines, sits at each end on files d–f: ranks 1–3 for the bottom player (Red) and ranks 8–10 for the top player (Black). Files are labelled a–i left to right; ranks are numbered 1–10 from Red’s back rank.

### Pieces

Each player commands 16 pieces in seven types. Red and Black use differently named equivalents of the same piece types.

| Piece | Red | Black | Count | Movement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| General | 帥 | 將 | 1 | One step horizontally or vertically within the palace only. Cannot face the opposing General on an open file (Flying General rule) |
| Advisor | 仕 | 士 | 2 | One step diagonally within the palace only |
| Elephant | 相 | 象 | 2 | Exactly two steps diagonally. Blocked if a piece occupies the intermediate point. Cannot cross the river |
| Horse | 傌 | 馬 | 2 | One step orthogonally then one step diagonally outward. Blocked if a piece occupies the adjacent orthogonal point (hobbling the horse) |
| Chariot | 俥 | 車 | 2 | Any number of points horizontally or vertically. The most powerful piece |
| Cannon | 砲 | 炮 | 2 | Moves like a Chariot when not capturing. To capture, must jump over exactly one intervening piece (the screen) of either colour; any number of empty points are permitted on either side of the screen |
| Soldier | 兵 | 卒 | 5 | One point forward before crossing the river. After crossing the river, may also move one point sideways. Cannot move backward |

### Special Rules

**Flying General:** The two Generals may never face each other on the same file with no intervening piece. Any move that creates this situation is illegal, even if the piece that moved is not the General. A player may use this constraint offensively to force a winning position.

**Stalemate is a loss:** A player with no legal moves on their turn loses the game. This differs from Western chess, where stalemate is a draw.

**Perpetual check:** The checking side must break the cycle of repeated checks or loses by repetition.

**Perpetual chase:** Repeatedly chasing an unprotected piece is restricted under tournament rules. The chasing side must vary the pattern or lose by repetition.

### Win Condition

Checkmate the opponent’s General: place it under attack with no legal escape. The General must either move to a safe square, block the attack, or capture the attacker. If none of these options is available, it is checkmate.
