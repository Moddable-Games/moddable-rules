## Atlantic City

The Atlantic City variant uses the rules of the 1906 Commercial Edition with one difference: the property names are replaced with the names of Atlantic City streets. The version was created by Jesse and Eugene Raiford, members of a Quaker community in Atlantic City, New Jersey, who hand-copied The Landlord's Game and replaced the original lot names with local streets familiar to their community. It was one of many hand-copied versions circulating through the 1910s–1920s under various names.

Charles Darrow encountered this specific version at a dinner party in Atlantic City around 1933. He subsequently sold the game to Parker Brothers in 1935 under the name Monopoly, representing it as his own invention. The Atlantic City variant is therefore the direct ancestor of the world's best-selling commercial board game.

### Setup

As per the 1906 Commercial Edition.

### Rules

As per the 1906 Commercial Edition. All rules apply unchanged. The sole difference from the 1906 edition is the property names, which use Atlantic City street names in place of the original lot names.

The highest-value properties in the Raiford version are known to include Boardwalk and Park Place, consistent with the Atlantic City street geography of the era. The full board list of Atlantic City street names as used in the Raiford version has not survived as a published document; the hand-copied board was never formally published.

### Winning

As per the 1906 Commercial Edition: most wealth at end of game, counting Title Deed cards + houses + cash on hand.
